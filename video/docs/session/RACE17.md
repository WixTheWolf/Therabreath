# THE FLAVOR RACE V17: the cut you do not see

V16 had the story, the score and the sound. The notes on it: no text of any kind but the end credits; the shot at 0:15
with the Moon behind the two rockets is out; and the edit itself has to be as good as we can make it, every cut paced,
nothing jumpy, the kind of edit where the audience never notices a cut. V17 keeps V16's structure, score and voice
and rebuilds the picture cut by cut on the rules below, with the sound rebuilt to carry the audience across each cut.

## What the research said (sources at the end)
1. Continuity editing is the craft of hiding the seam. Its tools are old and specific: cut on action, keep screen
   direction, change the angle by more than thirty degrees when you stay on the same subject, match eyelines, and let
   the eye already be where the next important thing appears.
2. Murch's rule of six ranks what a cut must serve: emotion (about half the weight), story, rhythm, eye trace, the
   two-dimensional plane, the three-dimensional space. Work the list from the top; a cut that is right in emotion and
   rhythm survives a small spatial sin, never the reverse.
3. Murch on where to cut inside a movement: at the moment the action becomes inevitable, so the new angle shows it
   finishing. Not half-formed. He cuts a frame or two before a blink and never on the blink. His test is dynamic
   trimming: play the shot at speed and hit the cut live; if you cannot hit the same frame twice, the rhythm is wrong.
4. Pearlman: rhythm is the shaping of the energy in the shots into one continuous flow; physical rhythm creates
   kinesthetic empathy. The energy curve of the whole film matters more than any one cut: build into the launch, open
   out in space, quicken through the discovery, slow on the Moon, settle at home.
5. Invisible cuts in practice: hide the cut inside movement. Match the direction, the speed and the blur across the
   cut (the whip-pan guides say match direction, speed, lens, shutter and the brightness of the blur, with three to six
   frames of blur each side). Other hiding places: a foreground object passing the lens, a flash, a very dark or very
   bright frame, a subject filling the frame.
6. Sound makes the cut felt, not seen. J cuts (the incoming sound leads the picture) and L cuts (the outgoing sound
   trails it) are the standard way to carry attention across an edit; a continuous ambience bed joins clips that look
   different; audio perspective stays consistent; diegetic sound irons over awkward cuts. Start with about half a
   second of overlap.
7. Dissolves: the cut is the default. A dissolve says time has passed or the mood is turning, and a twenty to thirty
   frame dissolve forgives timing that a hard cut would expose. Use them for passages, never for energy.
8. Music: cuts land on the strong beats at the turning points, not on every beat; follow the phrases and the changes
   of energy. Cutting a hair before the beat lets the beat land inside the new shot.
9. Generated footage: trim the dead frames before the action; keep stable handles; let a moving element carry across
   adjacent clips; use occlusion, cutaways and a head-on shot to reset direction; inspect the frames around every cut
   at full size and normal speed; speed changes must keep believable weight.
10. The mute test: watch the cut with the sound off; a shot that runs past three seconds without new information is
    shortened or removed.

## The rules V17 cuts by
- No overlays at all but the end card. No shot whose point is its lettering (the SEARCHING screen, the FLAVOR
  TRANSFER READY button, THERABREATH ONE on the monitors). Incidental text that belongs to the scene (the labels on
  the bottles, the monitors behind Mission Control) stays as shot.
- No shot under one second unless the picture itself hides the cut (a flash, a whip, something crossing the lens).
- Every cut sits on movement, with the vector carried across (same direction, comparable speed), or it is a direction
  reset through a head-on shot, or a dissolve for a passage of time.
- A cut that stays on the same subject changes size by at least one shot size or angle by more than thirty degrees.
- The eye is led: the thing the audience is watching at the end of the outgoing shot is where the incoming shot puts
  its first important thing.
- Sound: a continuous bed per place (the pad, the cabin, the room, the lab, space, the Moon, New Jersey); the incoming
  place's bed arrives a third to a half second before the picture and the outgoing place's bed trails after it;
  whooshes only where the picture has a whip or a pass; the score's downbeats on the turning points.
- The energy curve: long shots and silence to open; shorter as the count runs; one held shot at the edge of space; the
  race at two to three seconds a shot; the montage on the score's bars; the lab slower; the Moon slow; home
  measured; the card still.

### What changed against V16, cut by cut
- Out: the title and its hold on black, the two supers, the SEARCHING screen, the FLAVOR TRANSFER READY button, the
  THERABREATH ONE monitors, the shot of both rockets against the Moon on the pad, the frosted mint (static), the shot
  of both rockets at full thrust before the pass (it now re-enters the race after the silence instead).
- Longer, so they read: the hero bottle on the pad (10 frames to 1.8 s), the umbilical (15 frames to 1.0 s, the
  release on "two"), the button (the press on "one"), the drop on the orange (19 frames to 1.6 s, with its crown), the
  relight (24 frames to 1.8 s, the engines catching on the score's return), the plasma (14 frames to 1.2 s), the
  pass (2.4 to 3.7 s, so the rival is seen falling behind), the lift-off for home, the re-entry, the lamps on the pad
  (out on the searchlights' sweep), the hull into the sun (the rival slides into frame at its end).
- Continuous takes where V16 cut the same clip against itself: the regolith into the footpad (the pad now lands where
  it meets the ground, at +1.45), the clouds into New Jersey, the Church & Dwight sign into the sky and the contrail
  (the camera's own whip up through the tree is the transition; the seal patch fades out as the whip begins), the
  empty flag into the rival's landing.
- Cuts hidden by the footage: the bottle filling the lens at the end of px26 (into the rival's engines); the ignition
  whiteout; the cut into the inside of the cloud.
- Cuts that no longer sit on the same composition: the chase ends before its pull-back (hull close into two rockets
  from a new side, not two rockets into two rockets); the relight cuts to the pass.
- Dissolves (centred on the cut, `xf` frames): up from black into the condensation (12), into the stripe (16), into
  the cap in steam (12), into the regolith (20), into the Earthrise (24), into black before the card (18).
- The montage runs six shots on the score's beats (three, three, four, three, four, seven); nothing under 1.17 s and
  every cut on its action (the pop, the burst, the drop, the split).
- The orbit shot is framed on the signal's trails (zoom 2.0, anchored left) so the fruit at their head stays out of
  frame; the cap in steam ends at 1.71 s, before its baked-in letters begin at 1.82.
- The text that remains is in the scene: the labels on the two bottles, the monitors behind Mission Control, the
  flag's wordmark, the Church & Dwight sign and seal, and the end card.

### Sound: `sound/cues17.py` (233 cues), `sound/vo17.py`, `sound/music17.py`, `sound/events17.py`
- One bed per place, and the beds bridge the cuts: the far machine and the venting run under all three macros and
  their dissolves; the room arrives a third of a second before Mission Control is seen and trails into the cold of
  the pad; the rival's idle rises under the end of the wide shot; the room rises under the dead engines (a J cut) and
  under the end of the pass; the lab is heard under the end of the yuzu; the evening air and the birds rise inside the
  cloud and own the picture as it breaks out.
- Whooshes only where the picture moves: the searchlights' sweep, the bottle filling the lens, the pass, the
  overtake, the whip up through the tree, the contrail across the sky.
- The count's three sounds sit on its three words (the heartbeat under each, the umbilical on "two", the click on
  "one"). The footpad lands where the pad meets the ground. The rival's engine sputters down out of the sky before it
  is in frame, lands at +2.2, and the line follows.
- The voice follows the picture's perspective: lines heard while we are in the room are clean; lines heard while we
  are with the rockets, at the launch site or on the Moon come over the air with the Quindar tones ("Look at her
  go!", "Go get it!" and "Bring it home" are now over the air; "Bring it home" rides the Earthrise and the lift-off
  answers it).
- The score: the same five cues and the same bar-line anchors as V16 (bar 5 on the first montage cut, bar 11 on the
  pipette, bar 19 on the relight to the frame, the launch cue's sag on the warning light); the discovery cue's bar 3
  now tiptoes in under the dead engines a beat before the room is seen; the finale's bar 29 sits on the lift-off for
  home so its last chord (file 67.0) lands on the lawn half a second after the touchdown (the home stretch is two bars
  longer than V16's).
