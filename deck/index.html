02Territories · the base

Built to survive oxygen.

Survives sodium chlorite

Fades or turns

Before any flavor, the base. TheraBreath is an oxygenating sodium chlorite rinse, and that changes the rules. In the lab, sodium chlorite is the classic reagent for turning aldehydes into acids. In a rinse it works slowly, but it works the same way. That’s why citrus, cinnamon, vanilla, clove and cucumber fade or turn in this kind of base, and why mint has had the shelf to itself. So we built every new flavor from molecules with nothing for the oxidizer to find: saturated alcohols like menthol, ethers like cineole, amide coolants, saturated lactones and esters. This is our bench read, and everything still goes into accelerated stability in the real base.

Objective four: the pipeline. Before anything goes in it, every flavor, tasted or created this morning, clears three checks. Does it fit the brand? Can we make it, in sodium chlorite, over the full shelf life? And will people choose it? Only the flavors that clear all three lead the near term.
Decision board

Six enter. Three lead the near term.

Let’s go flavor by flavor and tick a check only when the room agrees. Three lead the near term. If more than three clear all checks, we rank on the repeat-use score from the tasting. The rest aren’t lost: they move to Next or Long-term on the pipeline.
