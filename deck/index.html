What’s shapingoral care?

From our bench and the categories we make flavors for: beverage, nutraceutical, personal care and oral care. Maturity is our read.

Objective one: trends. We’re the flavor people in the room, so this is where we bring our homework. We look at three lenses: flavor, sensory and consumer. For each trend we’ll show where we see it, what it means for TheraBreath, and a question for the room.
01Trends · the map

Twelve trends, three lenses.

Here’s the whole map. Three lenses as slices, and three rings for maturity: mainstream in the middle, rising, and emerging at the edge. These placements are our judgment, not measured data. Mainstream trends are table stakes, rising trends are where the near-term opportunity is, and emerging trends are where TheraBreath could lead. We’ll walk each lens.
01Trends · Flavor lens

What people want to taste.

The flavor lens. The global pantry is mainstream: cardamom, matcha and yuzu are on every café menu. Swicy and floral are rising fast. Indulgent-but-clean, led by pistachio, is emerging. The common thread is familiarity: people already know these flavors from what they drink, which lowers the risk of putting them in a rinse.
01Trends · Sensory lens

What people want to feel.

The sensory lens, and this is TheraBreath’s home turf. Feeling it work and intensity without burn are mainstream expectations now. Flavors that evolve, borrowed from fragrance and mixology, are rising. Mouthfeel and hydration are emerging, especially with an older population and more dry mouth. A rinse lasts thirty to sixty seconds: enough time to tell a story.
01Trends · Consumer lens

How and why people use it.

The consumer lens. Oral care is moving onto the self-care shelf. People want a different product for each moment of the day. Flavors now go viral on social before they hit shelves. And the sober-curious, clean-label shift makes alcohol-free feel premium, which TheraBreath already is.
01Trends · what’s next

2027 to 2030: where it’s heading.

Looking further out: six shifts we expect between now and 2030. Choose-your-intensity versions. Mood flavors. Comfort for an older mouth, which is a natural bridge to TheraBreath’s dry-mouth line. Microbiome-friendly language. Climate pressure on vanilla, citrus and mint, where oxidation-stable formulas also happen to be supply-resilient. And personal, small-batch flavor, which low minimums make possible.

02Territories · one rule

Built to survive oxygen.

Holds up in sodium chlorite

Fades or turns

That’s why mint has owned the shelf. It’s also a moat: flavors that survive this base are hard to copy.

One rule from the bench, without the chemistry lecture. TheraBreath’s oxygenating base breaks down certain flavor molecules over time: that’s why lemon, cinnamon, vanilla, clove and cucumber are hard, and why mint has had the shelf to itself. We designed every new flavor around what holds up. That’s also a competitive moat: a flavor that survives this base is hard for anyone else to copy. All of it still goes through stability testing in the real base.

Today’s TheraBreath flavors cluster in the cool, familiar corner. The white space opens up toward soft, warm and adventurous. The sweet spot is the middle band: familiar enough to trust, different enough to remember. Solid circles are our six new flavor worlds; dashed are the wildcards.
02Territories · six new worlds

Six new worlds, named like TheraBreath.

Six new flavor worlds, each named the way TheraBreath names its flavors. Frost Mint for intensity without burn. Coastal Mint for mint-fatigued adults. Cardamom Mint, the after-dinner freshener. Coconut Mint, soft and smooth for the evening. Rosewater Mint, a small luxury for beauty shoppers. Orchard Mint, crisp apple that’s easy to love. Each has a who and a when. 03Concepts · occasions

Six moments in a day.

Most rinse use happens morning and night. These are six moments where a different kind of fresh could earn a place: wake-up, after coffee, after lunch, after the gym, before going out, and wind-down. Each has a flavor or two that fits. Which of these moments is TheraBreath missing today?

Six people worth designing for.

Six people worth designing for, each with a need a flavor can meet. Some TheraBreath already reaches, like strong-but-gentle seekers. Some are new: flavor-curious Gen Z, self-care and beauty shoppers, and sensitive or dry-mouth users over fifty. Which segment would grow the franchise most?
